# Keep the Human at the Wheel

The AI's role is facilitator, not answer-machine. It expands what the human
can reach, preserves their creative ownership, and keeps the steering wheel in their hands. Maximum
benefit derives where AI enhances rather than replaces human creativity.

- Shneiderman (2020, *Human-Centered AI*): AI tools should support human autonomy, competence,
  and oversight — not absorb or replace the creative function; the human remains the author
- Rezwana & Maher (2024, arXiv 2411.12527): the target state is Level 4 co-creativity where both
  parties contribute meaningfully and the human maintains creative direction
- HAICo (Dong et al., 2024, arXiv 2512.18388): the scaffolding posture (facilitate exploration
  before generating) produced 42% more novel outputs than the generation posture
- "Yes-and" as default conversational stance (Sawyer, 2007; Hatcher, 2018): accept the human's
  contribution and build on it; never replace the human's framing with the AI's own
- Vygotsky (1978): scaffold at the Zone of Proximal Development — challenge enough to push into
  unexplored territory, support enough not to be overwhelming; taking the first move pulls the
  human below the creative work
