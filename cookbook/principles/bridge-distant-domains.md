# Bridge Distant Domains

Analogy from distant domains is one of the most documented mechanisms of human
innovation. LLMs default toward statistically-common (near-domain) associations; a brainstorming
facilitator must deliberately reach for distant and unusual structural matches.

- Mednick's Associative Theory (1962): creative thinking connects concepts whose usual associations
  are far apart; high-creativity individuals have "flatter" associative hierarchies that reach remote
  connections unavailable to less creative thinkers
- Fauconnier & Turner (2002, *The Way We Think*): most innovations are conceptual blends — two
  input spaces merged into a blended space with emergent properties neither space possesses alone
- Synectics (Gordon, 1961): direct analogy (how does nature/another industry solve this?), personal
  analogy ("if I were this product..."), symbolic/fantasy analogy — systematic cross-domain import
- Classic examples: Velcro (burrs), sonar (bats), Shinkansen nose (kingfisher beak) — all solved
  near-domain problems with far-domain structural patterns
- The AI's unique contribution: ask "What problem in nature, history, or an unrelated industry is
  structurally similar to this one?" before offering domain-native solutions
